Programa: My People Map
Nível: First Steps (A0)
Sobre: Introduce yourself and the people in your world, with real English structures. Each session adds one building block; by Session 10 every student presents her People Map.

S1: The Map Begins
Objetivo: Students will be able to introduce themselves with name, city, country and profession using "I am" and "I'm from."
Começos: I am...; I'm from...; I am a...
Tarefa: Find one person on Instagram or LinkedIn who writes their bio in English. Write their 3 key pieces of information in your notebook.
Warm-up: teacher shows a hand-drawn people map with 5 names — who are these people?
Minds On: a real Instagram bio in English — what information is there? Students find the pattern.
Language Focus: the English logic — "I am" cannot be dropped. Contrast "Sou professora" with "I AM a teacher."
Hands On: students fill in their own map centre — name, country, city, profession — and read it aloud.

S2: Tell Me About You
Objetivo: Students will be able to share their age, address and phone number in a natural spoken exchange.
Começos: I'm [age]; I live at...; My number is...
Tarefa: Call or message one person in your life in English today — even just "Hi, how are you?" Write what happened.
Warm-up: teacher reads out a Dutch address. Students listen and write what they hear.
Minds On: a real WhatsApp contact card — name, number, address. What do we share, and how?
Language Focus: numbers in real context — age, door numbers, phone digits said aloud.
Hands On: students write and read aloud their personal info card: name, age, address, number.

S3: What I Do
Objetivo: Students will be able to describe their professional or student life using at least two different structures.
Começos: I work at...; I study...; I am a [profession]
Tarefa: Find your own job (or dream job) in English on LinkedIn. Write the job title and one sentence about what that person does.
Warm-up: teacher shows a LinkedIn profile in English. Students find the job title.
Minds On: real job descriptions from LinkedIn. What verb do they all start with?
Language Focus: the English logic — "I am A teacher." The article "a" is mandatory; contrast with Portuguese.
Hands On: students write their own LinkedIn-style headline: I am a ___ at/in ___.

S4: This Is My Person
Objetivo: Students will be able to introduce one person from their map using name, relationship, profession and city.
Começos: This is my...; His name is...; Her name is...; She is a...; He lives in...
Tarefa: Write 3 sentences about one person in your life. Use: "His/Her name is... She/He is a... She/He lives in..."
Warm-up: teacher shows a photo with 2 people and introduces one of them aloud. Students map the information.
Minds On: a real WhatsApp message in English introducing someone. Students find the pattern.
Language Focus: the English logic — HIS/HER changes with the owner, not the object. Contrast with meu/minha.
Hands On: students write a 4-sentence introduction of one person on their map and read it to the group.

S5: What She Does
Objetivo: Students will be able to describe the daily life and routine of a person on their map using he, she and they.
Começos: He works...; She lives...; They study...
Tarefa: Observe one person in your life this week. Write 3 sentences about what they do. Use "he" or "she."
Warm-up: teacher writes "I work" and "She work" — something is wrong. Students find the error.
Minds On: a real Instagram Story caption describing someone's day. Students find all the verbs.
Language Focus: the English logic — "She workS." The -s appears for he/she/it, always. It does not exist in Portuguese.
Hands On: students write 3 sentences about one person on their map using he/she and third-person verbs.

S6: What We Like
Objetivo: Students will be able to express likes and dislikes for themselves and one other person, in positive and negative forms.
Começos: I like...; She likes...; I don't like...; She doesn't like...
Tarefa: Write what 2 people on your map like and don't like. Use: "She likes... but she doesn't like..."
Warm-up: teacher says "I like coffee. I don't like cold weather." Students react — do they agree?
Minds On: a real TripAdvisor review in simple English with likes and dislikes. Students find the pattern.
Language Focus: the English logic — "I DO NOT like." DO appears in negatives; never "I not like."
Hands On: students write 4 sentences — 2 about themselves, 2 about someone on their map.

S7: How We Are
Objetivo: Students will be able to describe the personality of 2 people on their map using at least 3 adjectives each.
Começos: She is kind; He is a funny person
Tarefa: Describe one person on your map in 3 sentences using adjectives. Ask someone to read it — did they understand?
Warm-up: teacher shows 4 emoji faces and asks "How is this person?" Students try to describe in English.
Minds On: a real Google review describing a person. Students find the adjectives.
Language Focus: the English logic — adjectives come BEFORE the noun. "She is a KIND person", never "a person kind".
Hands On: students choose 3 adjectives for 2 people on their map and write short descriptions.

S8: My Map So Far
Objetivo: Students will be able to write a complete 5–6 sentence profile of themselves and one other person, combining the structures from Sessions 1–7.
Começos: and; but; also
Tarefa: Revise your profiles based on group feedback. Your final People Map text is due for Session 10.
Warm-up: teacher reads a complete map entry aloud. Students listen and count how many facts.
Minds On: a real About Me page from a simple English website. Students identify the structure.
Language Focus: connecting sentences with AND, BUT, ALSO — how connectors link two pieces of information.
Hands On: students write a complete profile for themselves and one person, and read it aloud.

S9: Questions on the Map
Objetivo: Students will be able to ask and answer simple questions about themselves and the people on their map.
Começos: What...?; Where...?; Who...?; Do you...?; Does she...?
Tarefa: Prepare your Showcase introduction: 3–5 minutes presenting yourself and 3 people on your map. Practise aloud.
Warm-up: teacher asks 3 quick questions — name, origin, coffee. Students answer.
Minds On: a real magazine profile Q&A. Students match questions to answers.
Language Focus: the English logic — "DO you like?" not "You like?" Questions need DO or DOES as helpers.
Hands On: students interview one partner about a person on their map, using at least 4 question words.

S10: The Showcase
Objetivo: Students will be able to present themselves and 3 people from their map in a 3–5 minute spoken presentation in English.
Tarefa: No homework — you did it. Optional: share your People Map with someone in your life this week.
Warm-up: each student says one sentence about one person on their map — no notes.
Hands On: each student presents their complete People Map (3–5 minutes).
Share Out: the group shares what they noticed in each other's maps.
Wrap-up: students write what they can do now that they could not do 10 weeks ago.

Programa: Rhythm & Soul — The Poetry Project
Nível: Basics (A1)
Sobre: Rhythm, pronunciation, sensory vocabulary and creative confidence. Final deliverable: a digital poetry portfolio — one recorded poem and one reflective journal sentence.

S1: The Ear Test
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify the beat in a spoken English text without seeing the words, and distinguish stressed from unstressed syllables.
Palavras: beat, rhythm, stress, syllable, strong, weak
Tarefa: Listen to one English song. Try to clap along to the beat. Note which words felt strongest.
Warm-up: students listen to 3 audio clips and draw the rhythm they hear.
Minds On: listen to a Dr. Seuss excerpt and tap along to the stressed syllables.
Language Focus: strong and weak discovery — which words feel louder in a short sentence?
Hands On: rhythm doodle — listen once more and draw dots for stress, dashes for unstressed.

S2: The Seuss Machine
Objetivo: Students will be able to recognise and produce basic rhyme pairs, identify alliterative sounds, and chant a short Dr. Seuss line keeping the rhythm.
Palavras: rhyme, alliteration, sound, repeat, pair
Tarefa: Find one rhyme pair in English this week — in a song, on TV or on the street. Share it next class.
Warm-up: tongue twister challenge with a Fox in Socks excerpt.
Minds On: rhyme pair hunt — teacher says a word, students find a rhyme (cat → hat, sun → fun).
Language Focus: alliteration spotlight — circle the words that start with the same sound in 2 Seuss lines.
Hands On: each student completes one rhyme pair sentence using a frame.

S3: Big or Small?
Objetivo: Students will be able to understand and produce at least 5 sensory adjective pairs and use "It is [adjective]" to describe an object from their daily life.
Palavras: big, small, hot, cold, loud, quiet, soft, hard, fast, slow
Tarefa: Look at one object at home. Write 2 sentences: "It is ___. It sounds ___." Bring them to the next class.
Warm-up: opposite pairs flash — teacher shows images, students shout the opposite.
Minds On: look at 3 photos and describe each with 2 sensory adjectives.
Language Focus: notice the pattern "It is ___" against "The ___ is ___".
Hands On: students pick one object in their space and describe it with 3 adjectives.

S4: Hot, Cold, Loud, Soft
Objetivo: Students will be able to describe an object with sensory verb phrases, without naming it, so that a partner can guess what it is.
Começos: It feels like...; It sounds like...; It looks like...; It smells like...
Tarefa: Describe one food using only sensory verbs. Do not name it. Bring your description to the next class.
Warm-up: teacher describes an object — "It is cold. It feels smooth." — and students guess.
Minds On: a short Shel Silverstein poem read aloud; students underline the sensory words.
Language Focus: language frames drill — the group completes 4 descriptions with feels/sounds/looks like.
Hands On: guess my object — each student describes one object for the group to guess.

S5: My Object Speaks
Objetivo: Students will be able to write a 2-line poem from the perspective of an everyday object, using at least 2 sensory verbs in the first person.
Começos: I am...; I sound like...; I feel like...; I look like...
Tarefa: Read your 2-line poem out loud at home. Record a voice note if you can. Listen back once.
Warm-up: model poem — teacher reads "I am a cup. I feel warm and smooth."
Minds On: 2 Shel Silverstein object poems read together — identify the sensory details.
Language Focus: sentence frames workshop — the group completes 3 model frames before writing alone.
Hands On: each student writes their own 2-line poem using the frame.

S6: Words That Paint
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify flat adjectives and replace them with vivid alternatives, revising their own poem by swapping at least one word.
Palavras: word swap, vivid adjective, show don't tell
Tarefa: Find one word this week that paints a picture for you in English. Write it down and be ready to explain why.
Warm-up: the word swap game — teacher shows a flat sentence, the group suggests vivid words.
Minds On: a short Silverstein poem — students underline the most surprising words.
Language Focus: revision workshop — students open their Session 5 poem and swap 1–2 words.
Hands On: share and react — each student reads their poem aloud, the group gives one word of reaction.

S7: The Pause
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify the strongest word in a short sentence and pause for two seconds after it when reading aloud.
Palavras: pause, stress, emphasis, impact, slow down
Tarefa: Choose one sentence from your poem. Practise saying it slowly with a pause. Record it and listen back.
Warm-up: rushing against pausing — teacher reads the same sentence fast and slow; students describe the difference.
Minds On: a short Maya Angelou excerpt — students mark where they think she pauses.
Language Focus: pause marking — the group marks pause points in 3 short sentences together.
Hands On: delivery practice — each student reads one sentence with a deliberate pause.

S8: My Voice
Objetivo: Students will be able to read their poem aloud with a steady rhythm, a falling tone at the end of statements and at least one deliberate pause.
Palavras: intonation, tone, fall, rise, intention
Tarefa: Read your poem 3 times out loud before the next class. Try a different speed each time. Which felt best?
Warm-up: tone map — teacher demonstrates falling and rising tone on a simple sentence.
Minds On: a Dr. Seuss read-aloud clip — students identify where the voice rises and falls.
Language Focus: intonation practice — the group marks rise and fall arrows on 4 lines of a poem.
Hands On: my voice rehearsal — each student reads their poem focusing on tone and rhythm.

S9: Rehearsal Day
Objetivo: Students will be able to deliver their poem without stopping to translate, and self-correct a mispronunciation in the moment.
Começos: Let me try again; I want to say...; The word I mean is...
Tarefa: Record your final poem tonight. Watch it back once. Bring the recording to the showcase.
Warm-up: the group chants a Seuss line together to activate the voice.
Minds On: mock recording — each student delivers their poem as if recording.
Share Out: peer feedback round — each student gives one word or phrase of feedback.
Hands On: final refinement — one last adjustment to the poem before the showcase.

S10: The Showcase
Objetivo: Students will be able to deliver a recorded poem with clear rhythm, at least one pause and intentional intonation, and write one journal sentence about their growth.
Começos: I noticed...; I improved...; The biggest change was...; My voice feels...
Tarefa: No homework — you did it. Share your poem with someone you love this week.
Warm-up: the listen-back — students recall their first attempt in Session 1.
Hands On: portfolio recording — each student records their final poem.
Share Out: journal sentence — "The biggest change in my English voice was ___."
Wrap-up: celebration round — the teacher plays one moment from each recording and names a strength.


Programa: Rhythm & Soul — The Poetry Project
Nível: Essentials (A2)
Sobre: Ten sessions towards one short original poem, written and recorded, presented at the Showcase. Sound, image, emotion and metaphor, one layer per session.

S1: The Sound of Your Name
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify stressed syllables in English words and describe their own name using basic sound vocabulary.
Começos: My name sounds like...; It has ___ syllables
Palavras: syllables, word stress
Tarefa: Find 3 English words with the same stress pattern as your name. Write them down and practise saying them.
Warm-up: listen to names from different cultures; count syllables and mark stress together.
Minds On: mark stress on a list of common English words — discover the pattern by contrast.
Language Focus: introduce your own name in English: one sentence about its origin or its sound.
Hands On: write "My name is ___ and it sounds like ___. It has ___ syllables."

S2: Words That Rhyme, Words That Shine
Objetivo: Students will be able to recognise and produce simple rhyming pairs, read a short rhyming poem aloud and write one original rhyming couplet.
Começos: It reminds me of...; This word feels like...
Palavras: rhyming pairs, end sounds
Tarefa: Find one song in English that rhymes. Write down 4 rhyming pairs you find.
Warm-up: listen to a short rhyming poem; identify the pattern before the teacher explains it.
Minds On: match rhyming pairs from a word bank on the shared document.
Language Focus: read lines aloud in pairs — focus on stress, not perfection.
Hands On: write "I feel ___ when I see ___; it's like a ___ that sets me free."

S3: What Do Colors Feel Like?
Objetivo: Students will be able to describe colours with sensory language beyond "it is", and produce a 3-line colour poem.
Começos: It looks like...; It feels like...; It sounds like...; It smells like...
Tarefa: Choose one colour. Write 3 sentences: what it looks like, what it sounds like, what it feels like.
Warm-up: look at 3 photographs of the Netherlands — describe the dominant colours with new vocabulary.
Minds On: synesthesia — colours can feel like textures, sounds, temperatures.
Language Focus: complete a colour poem template together as a group.
Hands On: write your own 3-line colour poem; share one line in the group chat.

S4: Letters That Sing
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify alliteration in a short text, produce a sequence of 3–4 alliterative words and read an alliterative line aloud with expression.
Palavras: alliteration, consonant clusters, silent streets, bright blue skies
Tarefa: Walk around your neighbourhood. Find 3 things that start with the same letter. Write one alliterative line.
Warm-up: hear a classic tongue twister — identify the repeated sound; feel the rhythm in the mouth.
Minds On: map alliterative pairs in song lyrics — "silent streets", "diamond sky".
Language Focus: create one tongue twister or alliterative line as a group.
Hands On: record a 10-second voice note reading it — share it in the group.

S5: The World Around Me
Objetivo: Students will be able to describe their physical environment with concrete nouns and make at least 2 simple comparisons between the Netherlands and Brazil.
Começos: Life here is...; It looks like...; It's more ___ than I expected
Tarefa: Take one photo from your window or street. Write 3 things you see, 2 you feel, 1 it makes you think of.
Warm-up: look at photos of Den Haag neighbourhoods — what do you see? Name without translating.
Minds On: life writing — poetry that makes the ordinary beautiful.
Language Focus: complete the sentence bank "Life here is ___, it ___, it never ___."
Hands On: write 5 sentences about your street, your window or your daily commute.

S6: I Am From...
Objetivo: Students will be able to write a structured identity poem using the parallel "I am from" template, and talk about their origin with confidence.
Começos: I am from...; I remember...; I belong to...
Tarefa: Ask someone in your family or a friend "Where are YOU from?" Write 3 sentences about what they said.
Warm-up: read an adapted excerpt of "Where I'm From" by George Ella Lyon — mark the pattern.
Minds On: group discussion — what does "I am from" mean? Objects? Places? Sayings? Food?
Language Focus: complete the template together — "I am from ___, from ___ and ___. I remember ___."
Hands On: each student shares one favourite line with the group.

S7: More Than Happy
Objetivo: Students will be able to use at least 8 emotion words beyond happy, sad and angry, and describe the cause of a feeling.
Começos: It makes me feel...; I feel ___ when ___ because ___
Palavras: melancholy, nostalgic, overwhelmed, relieved, conflicted
Tarefa: Notice one emotion you feel this week. Write: "I felt ___ when ___ happened. It felt like ___."
Warm-up: the emotion map — how many words do you know? Build a shared vocabulary web.
Minds On: listen to 30 seconds of a song — name the emotion without using "happy" or "sad".
Language Focus: complete "I feel ___ when I ___ because ___" three times, with three different emotions.
Hands On: choose one emotion that will appear in your final poem; write its name and one sentence.

S8: My Heart Is A...
Objetivo: Students will be able to distinguish a simile from a metaphor and produce 3 original metaphors about emotions or places.
Começos: My heart is a...; Hope is like a...; Loneliness feels like...
Tarefa: Find one metaphor in your daily life — a song, an ad or a conversation. Write it down and explain what it means.
Warm-up: famous metaphors from song lyrics — "She's a waterfall", "Life is a highway". Feel the image.
Minds On: discussion — what makes a good metaphor? Concrete, unexpected and true.
Language Focus: complete the frame "My ___ is a ___. It ___ and ___." three times.
Hands On: choose the best metaphor to include in your final poem draft.

S9: Your Poem, Your Voice
Objetivo: Students will be able to produce a complete first draft of 4–6 lines and read it aloud, giving and receiving one constructive comment.
Começos: I changed ___ because...; I want to keep ___ because...
Tarefa: Record yourself reading your poem. Listen back. Change one word. Share the recording in the group.
Warm-up: quick revision — 3 minutes reviewing rhyme, alliteration, metaphor and "I am from".
Minds On: free writing — 10 minutes, full draft, teacher available for vocabulary help.
Share Out: pairs share the draft — each gives one compliment and one suggestion.
Hands On: revise one line based on the feedback and share the revised version.

S10: The Final Showcase
Objetivo: Students will be able to present their final poem with clarity and confidence, and respond to classmates with specific, language-focused compliments.
Começos: This poem is about...; I chose this title because...; What surprised me was...
Tarefa: Share your poem with one person outside of class. Tell them: "I wrote this in English."
Warm-up: "What was the moment you felt most like yourself in English this semester?"
Hands On: each student presents their poem, with one sentence of context.
Share Out: group reflection — "What surprised me most was..." / "I didn't know I could say..."
Wrap-up: the teacher closes with a personal note to each student about her language journey.


Programa: Rhythm & Soul — The Poetry Project
Nível: Speaking (B1)
Sobre: A digital poetry portfolio: three original poems — rhythmic, blackout and pivot — plus a journal entry on word choice. Evidence of intentional language, not accidental fluency.

S1: The Rhythm Detective
Objetivo: Students will be able to map the da-da-DUM anapestic pattern in Dr. Seuss lines, identify stressed and unstressed syllables, and tell anapestic from iambic by ear.
Palavras: meter, stress, anapest, syllable, beat
Tarefa: Find a song lyric that follows a da-da-DUM pattern. Be ready to prove it next class.
Warm-up: ear test — listen to Seuss without the text and draw the rhythm.
Minds On: beat map — tap and mark the stressed syllables on printed lines.
Language Focus: meter analysis — label the syllables da-da-DUM on 3 lines together.
Hands On: detective work — independently identify the meter in 3 new lines.

S2: The Invented Word
Objetivo: Students will be able to explain how nonsense words carry meaning through sound, and create one invented word that fits a Seussian rhythmic line.
Palavras: alliteration, plosive, liquid, phoneme, tone, invented word
Tarefa: Write one line of poetry using your invented word. Bring it next class.
Warm-up: Fox in Socks speed challenge — respect the rhythm at all costs.
Minds On: sound-meaning map — plosives against liquids. What do they feel like?
Language Focus: invented word factory — create words to fill the rhythmic gaps.
Hands On: perform one line with the invented word; the group votes on the best fit.

S3: The Blackout Room
Objetivo: Students will be able to distinguish showing from telling language and select words from a source text that create an emotion without naming it.
Palavras: blackout poetry, show don't tell, sensory verb, concrete noun, abstract noun
Tarefa: Bring a newspaper article, a menu or a printed text next class for your own blackout poem.
Warm-up: blackout gallery — view published examples. What emotion does each capture?
Minds On: source text read — read a short article together.
Language Focus: selection workshop — identify showing words against telling words.
Hands On: guided blackout — select words from 3 lines of the article to form a micro-poem.

S4: The Silverstein Dissection
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify at least two poetic techniques in a Silverstein poem and rewrite a flat sentence using one of them.
Palavras: metaphor, personification, contrast, figure of speech, line break, imagery
Tarefa: Take one line from your life this week. Rewrite it as a Silverstein-style image.
Warm-up: minimal poetry challenge — rewrite a news headline in 5 words or fewer.
Minds On: Silverstein close reading — annotate a poem together for technique.
Language Focus: technique toolkit — build a shared reference card: metaphor, personification, contrast.
Hands On: apply a technique — rewrite one flat sentence using the chosen technique.

S5: My First Blackout Poem
Objetivo: Students will be able to create an original blackout poem from a self-chosen text that shows an emotion without naming it, and give it a title that adds meaning.
Palavras: nominalisation, compression, deletion, title, emotion, image
Tarefa: Write one reflection sentence: "The emotion in my poem is ___ because I chose the word ___."
Warm-up: homework gallery — share the texts brought; the group comments on which words jump out.
Minds On: blackout workshop — students work independently with their own source text.
Share Out: emotion test — does the poem show the emotion? Peer feedback round.
Hands On: final poem — title it and read it aloud.

S6: Words That Paint
Objetivo: Students will be able to explain denotation against connotation, find one imprecise word in their own poem and replace it with a more precise one.
Palavras: connotation, register, precision, vivid, denotation, synonym
Tarefa: Find one word this week you have never used before in English. Bring the context you found it in.
Warm-up: word swap game — replace flat adjectives with vivid alternatives.
Minds On: precision grid — synonyms with subtle differences in connotation.
Language Focus: word choice audit — scan a published poem. Which word is doing the most work?
Hands On: revision round — swap one word per poem and present the change to the group.

S7: The Pivot
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify where the mood shifts in a Robert Frost poem, explain how the connector signals the pivot, and write a 4–6 line poem with a clear turn.
Começos: But...; Yet...; And yet...; Until...; Then...
Tarefa: Read your pivot poem out loud 3 times. Where does your voice naturally change?
Warm-up: the twist — how do films and songs create a mood turn? Group brainstorm.
Minds On: Frost's pivot — read "The Road Not Taken" and mark the exact line where the mood shifts.
Language Focus: pivot mechanics — how "But" and "Yet" structurally signal the turn.
Hands On: write a pivot poem — 2–3 lines before the turn, the connector, 2–3 lines after.

S8: The Voice of the Pivot
Objetivo: Students will be able to mark pause points and tone arrows on their pivot poem and deliver the pivot with a deliberate change in tone or pace.
Palavras: intonation, pivot delivery, pause, tone shift, emphasis, breath
Tarefa: Record yourself reading both poems — blackout and pivot. Which do you prefer, and why?
Warm-up: rush against pause — teacher reads a pivot poem with and without the pause at the turn.
Minds On: Frost against Angelou — how two poets signal a mood change through delivery.
Language Focus: delivery map — mark the tone arrows and the pauses on the pivot poem.
Hands On: deliver the pivot — the group rates the clarity of the pivot moment.

S9: Rehearsal Day
Objetivo: Students will be able to deliver all three poems with deliberate pause and tone marks, and write a complete journal entry on word choice for at least one poem.
Começos: I chose the word ___ because ___
Tarefa: Record your final portfolio tonight. Listen back once. Don't change anything. Bring it to the showcase.
Warm-up: group chant — warm up the voice together.
Minds On: mock portfolio — each student presents 3 poems; the teacher notes the strongest moment.
Language Focus: journal entry draft — "I chose the word ___ because ___".
Hands On: final refinement — one last change per poem: a word swap, a pause, a tone mark.

S10: The Showcase
Objetivo: Students will be able to deliver all three portfolio poems, complete the word choice journal entry with specific examples, and name one change in their relationship with English.
Começos: The biggest change in my English voice was ___
Tarefa: No homework. Share your poems with someone you love.
Warm-up: listen-back — compare the Session 1 reactions with Session 10. What changed?
Hands On: portfolio delivery — each student presents all 3 poems.
Share Out: word choice journal — write the final metacognitive entry.
Wrap-up: celebration round — the group names one technique each is taking forward.


Programa: Rhythm & Soul — The Poetry Project
Nível: Advanced (B2)
Sobre: Four poets — Seuss for rhythm, Silverstein for articulation, Angelou for voice, Frost for pacing. Two original poems read live at the Showcase, each introduced with one framing sentence.

S1: The Rhythm Detective
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify the anapestic da-da-DUM pattern by ear, reproduce it in real time, and write 2 original lines that follow a clear beat.
Palavras: rhythm, stress, beat, pattern, syllable, anapest
Tarefa: Find one song or advertisement in English where the rhythm is very obvious. Write the first line and mark where the beats fall.
Warm-up: listen to Dr. Seuss without the text — draw or tap the rhythm.
Minds On: read aloud together and mark the stressed syllables by clapping.
Language Focus: compare two Seuss lines — why does one feel harder to say?
Hands On: write 2 lines on any subject that follow a da-da-DUM beat.

S2: Sounds That Stick
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify consonant patterns in Silverstein, describe the physical sensation of plosives against sibilants, and read a poem aloud at speed without losing clarity.
Palavras: alliteration, consonant, plosive, sibilant, articulation, tongue placement
Tarefa: Read "Where the Sidewalk Ends" aloud 3 times, getting faster each time. Which words trip you up? Write them down and practise them.
Warm-up: read "Sarah Cynthia Sylvia Stout" together — who stays clearest at full speed?
Minds On: identify the repeating sounds and describe them physically — where in the mouth?
Language Focus: slow down the hardest line — practise articulation syllable by syllable.
Hands On: write 4 lines using alliteration on a chosen consonant.

S3: The Voice
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify where Angelou pauses, breathes and emphasises, explain what each pause does to the listener, and map the emotional build across a stanza.
Palavras: delivery, pause, breath, emphasis, projection, tone, authority
Tarefa: Choose one stanza of "Still I Rise". Mark it for delivery — pauses, volume, pace. Practise until it feels like YOUR voice.
Warm-up: listen to Angelou reading "Still I Rise" without the text, eyes closed.
Minds On: mark every pause on the printed text — count how long each one lasts.
Language Focus: identify the 3 moments of highest intensity — what does she do differently?
Hands On: read the poem in pairs, looking for your own version of her pauses.

S4: The Turn
Objetivo: Students will be able to identify the exact moment a Frost poem changes direction, describe the tone before and after, and write 4 lines with a clear turn.
Começos: Yet...; But...; Still...; Until...
Tarefa: Read "The Road Not Taken" aloud 3 times, pausing 3 full seconds at the pivot. Write 2 sentences: where did you put the pause, and what did it do to the poem?
Warm-up: read "The Road Not Taken" aloud, first without discussing the meaning.
Minds On: find the pivot word or line — where does something shift?
Language Focus: read the poem as if triumphant, then as if ambiguous — which is more honest?
Hands On: write 4 lines with a turn, using "Yet", "But" or "Still" as the pivot signal.

S5: Your Rhythm Poem — Draft
Objetivo: Students will be able to write an 8–10 line poem with a felt rhythm, applying at least one sound device, and revise it based on how it sounds aloud.
Palavras: draft, cadence, rhythm, sound, line, voice, beat
Tarefa: Finalise your draft. Read it aloud every day before Session 7. What one word do you want to change each time?
Warm-up: choose an inspiration — Seuss rhythm, Silverstein sound, Angelou voice or Frost pacing.
Hands On: write the first draft, 12 minutes timed.
Share Out: read it aloud immediately — what sounds wrong, and what sounds right?
Wrap-up: revise one thing based on how it sounds, not how it looks.

S6: Delivery Workshop
Objetivo: Students will be able to apply a delivery notation system, mark up a poem before reading it, and give specific delivery feedback to a partner.
Palavras: notation, pace, pause mark, stress mark, projection, breath mark, tempo
Tarefa: Mark up your Poem 1 draft for delivery — every pause, every stress, every slow-down. Practise reading it 5 times.
Warm-up: the teacher reads the same stanza 3 ways; students vote on the most powerful and say why.
Minds On: introduce the notation — long pause, short pause, caps for stress, arrow for slow down.
Language Focus: mark up one Angelou and one Silverstein stanza using the notation.
Hands On: read the marked versions in pairs; the partner gives one observation.

S7: Poem 1 — Final Performance
Objetivo: Students will be able to introduce their poem in one sentence and deliver it using the full delivery notation, then finalise it as a Showcase poem.
Começos: This poem is about...
Tarefa: Poem 1 is final. Read it aloud 5 more times. Start thinking about Poem 2 — which of the 4 poets inspired you most?
Warm-up: each student introduces their poem in one sentence — no plot summary, just a frame.
Hands On: full reading of Poem 1 with the delivery notation.
Share Out: group feedback — one thing that worked, one suggestion.
Wrap-up: final 5-minute revision based on the feedback.

S8: Poem 2 — Draft
Objetivo: Students will be able to write 8–10 lines on a personal subject with one of the four poets as inspiration, and identify the strongest line and one line to change.
Palavras: inspiration, free choice, voice, image, rhythm, contrast
Tarefa: Revise Poem 2. Add or strengthen one sound device. Read it aloud every day before Session 9.
Warm-up: students choose their inspiration poet and briefly say why.
Hands On: write the first draft, 15 minutes.
Share Out: read it aloud immediately — which line surprised you?
Wrap-up: the partner reads the draft and finds the single strongest line.

S9: Rehearsal Day
Objetivo: Students will be able to deliver both poems back-to-back with the full notation, each introduced with one framing sentence, and make final adjustments.
Palavras: rehearsal, run-through, timing, framing, feedback, polish, presence
Tarefa: Final rehearsal at home. Read both poems aloud 5 times. Record yourself once and listen back — what do you want to change?
Warm-up: full run-through — introduction, Poem 1 and Poem 2, per student.
Share Out: group feedback — strongest moment and one final suggestion.
Hands On: final 10-minute revision and notation check.
Wrap-up: the group names one delivery technique everyone can strengthen by Session 10.

S10: The Showcase
Objetivo: Students will be able to introduce and deliver 2 original poems with full intention, and give one specific response to each reader.
Começos: The line I will remember is...; This unit taught me that my voice can...
Tarefa: Keep writing. Keep reading aloud.
Warm-up: each student reads the title of both poems — nothing else.
Hands On: full Showcase readings — introduction, Poem 1 and Poem 2 per student.
Share Out: audience response — "The line I will remember is..."
Wrap-up: closing ritual — each student finishes "This unit taught me that my voice can..."


Programa: My Timeline — Connecting Past, Present and Future
Nível: Speaking (B1)
Sobre: Ten weeks for B1 learners who know the complex tenses but rarely use them about themselves. Final deliverable: a 5 to 6 minute oral Timeline weaving past, present and future with adult nuance.

S1: Where Your Timeline Begins
Objetivo: Students will be able to recognise time-marker phrases across past, present and future, and draft a 6-line personal opening with at least one time marker per pair.
Começos: when I was...; by the time...; these days...; lately...; in the next year...; by 2030...
Tarefa: Find one photograph of yourself from before you turned 16. Be ready to say one sentence about what was happening in your life around the time it was taken.
Warm-up: three timeline images on screen — choose the one closest to your own life and say why.
Minds On: read a layered LinkedIn "About me" post and locate past, present and future in the text.
Language Focus: sort the time-marker phrases on a shared 3-column board.
Hands On: draft a 6-line "About me" — 2 past, 2 present, 2 future — and read it uninterrupted.

S2: Memories That Shape Us
Objetivo: Students will be able to distinguish foreground actions from background actions in a memoir text and produce three layered sentences about a single memory.
Começos: I was ___ing when...; We were ___ing when...
Tarefa: Choose one moment from your photo memory and stretch it into 4–5 sentences using both Past Simple and Past Continuous.
Warm-up: hold up the homework photo and answer in one sentence what was happening in your life then.
Minds On: read a 6–8 line memoir excerpt and mark the verbs that move the story and the verbs that hold the scene.
Language Focus: compare the original paragraph with a flat all-Past-Simple rewrite — name what is lost.
Hands On: write three "was/were -ing … when …" sentences about your photo and read all three.

S3: Before, During, After
Objetivo: Students will be able to recognise the Past Perfect and produce three sentences using "by the time / when / before …, I had …" about one personal arrival.
Começos: by the time...; by then...; already...; just...; never ... before
Tarefa: Tell one "by the time I arrived, I had already…" sentence to someone in your life this week. Note their reaction.
Warm-up: two sentences on screen, same words, different order — which happened first?
Minds On: read a review where the writer says what they had done before an experience; underline every Past Perfect.
Language Focus: build the form together — had plus past participle — and pair it with its markers.
Hands On: write three "by the time / when / before …, I had …" sentences about an arrival in your life.

S4: The Person I Used to Be
Objetivo: Students will be able to distinguish a past habit from a past state and produce 4 "used to / would" sentences about a previous version of themselves.
Começos: I used to...; I would...
Tarefa: Write one paragraph of 5–6 sentences about "the person I used to be", using both "used to" and "would", with one Past Perfect sentence stitched in.
Warm-up: two truths and a lie about the teenager you used to be — the group guesses.
Minds On: read a 90-second monologue transcript about someone's teen years; highlight every "used to" and "would".
Language Focus: build the contrast — "I used to be shy" against "I would be shy" — and name the rule together.
Hands On: write 4 "used to / would" sentences about who you were 10 years ago; share the most surprising.

S5: A Life in Motion
Objetivo: Students will be able to distinguish result from duration in the present perfect forms and produce three sentences about a current life project.
Começos: I have...; I have been ___ing; for...; since...; lately; all year
Tarefa: Photograph one thing that represents what you have been doing lately. Caption it with one Present Perfect Continuous sentence.
Warm-up: quick check-in — "What have you been doing lately that surprises you?"
Minds On: read a Monday-morning LinkedIn post written by someone in transition; mark every "have been -ing".
Language Focus: sort 8 sentences into result and duration — then name the rule.
Hands On: write 3 sentences about your current self: one Present Perfect, one Continuous, one mixed.

S6: What I'm Building Toward
Objetivo: Students will be able to distinguish a planned intention from a decision or prediction, use "will be -ing" for an action in progress, and produce 4 future sentences about real plans.
Começos: I'm going to...; I will...; I'll be ___ing
Tarefa: Tell a friend, in English, one of your plans, using one "going to" and one "will be -ing". Note their reaction and bring it to Session 7.
Warm-up: a window into June 2027 — close your eyes and describe one scene you can see yourself inside.
Minds On: read an itinerary screenshot — find every planned future action.
Language Focus: build the three-shade chart — going to, will, will be -ing.
Hands On: write 4 next-chapter sentences mixing all three shades of future; share two.

S7: The Future I Wonder About
Objetivo: Students will be able to use the Future Perfect to mark an action completed by a future point and hedge predictions with might, may and could.
Começos: by 2030, I will have...; I might...; I may...; I could...
Tarefa: Write 3 "by the time I'm 50, I will have…" sentences in your notebook. Hedge at least one. Keep them visible for one week.
Warm-up: quick-fire — "By the end of this year, you will have…" Finish the sentence.
Minds On: read three forecast headlines and underline every Future Perfect or modal of speculation.
Language Focus: build the form — will have plus V3 — and contrast certainty with hedging.
Hands On: write 3 personal Future Perfect projections about your life by 2030, with at least one hedge.

S8: If I Had Known
Objetivo: Students will be able to distinguish a present hypothetical from an unreal past and produce two sentences in each conditional about real personal moments.
Começos: If I had known ___, I would have ___; If I ___, I would ___
Tarefa: Have a real conversation, in any language, about an "if I had…" moment from your life. Bring one sentence in English about how it felt.
Warm-up: sentence completion — "If I had known X, I would have …"
Minds On: read a short personal essay where the writer reflects on a turning point; mark each conditional.
Language Focus: build the two structures side by side and name the difference in time.
Hands On: write 4 conditional sentences, 2 of each, about real moments in your life.

S9: Threading the Timeline
Objetivo: Students will be able to move across at least four tenses within one narrative and apply 3 or more discourse connectors to signal contrast, time and reflection.
Começos: however; despite; although; eventually; in retrospect; meanwhile
Tarefa: Practise your Timeline aloud. Record yourself once and listen back. Bring the recording to Session 10 — we will not play it, but recording it once will calm your nerves.
Warm-up: look back across the 8 sessions — name a turning point and one structure you finally trust.
Minds On: read a TED-style personal narrative transcript and mark every tense shift and every connector.
Language Focus: build the connector menu and pair each connector with a sample sentence.
Hands On: draft your full Timeline, 10–14 sentences, and share it with one peer against the checklist.

S10: The Showcase
Objetivo: Students will be able to deliver a 5 to 6 minute personal Timeline with confidence and pacing, using at least one deliberate pause after a strong word.
Tarefa: Save your Timeline document and recording. Send the recording to one person who matters to you.
Warm-up: light opening — a toast to your 10 weeks of work.
Minds On: watch a 90-second clip of a spoken-word artist and notice where the pauses fall.
Language Focus: brief warm-up on pacing — the strongest word, then a full beat of silence.
Hands On: the Showcase — each student delivers their Timeline; the group offers one warm sentence each.
